The work described in this abstract is too preliminary for presentation. 
The topic is of course of interest and the pursued objective could provide 
some interesting insights on the understanding of the principles of human 
motion, especially in interaction with its environment. Nevertheless the 
attempt to provide quite a lot of information in two pages is unfortunate 
as the overall result is not very homogeneous in terms of the level of 
details. It mostly leads to confusion, especially as the proposed work 
mostly builds up on some unpublished work for which a preprint is not even 
available. Moreover, the number of subjects used for the experiments is 
too low to allow to draw any conclusion regarding the significance of the 
obtained results. Remarks such as  "We can see that for the experimented 
user, each cycle’s medians are closer than for the other users, which might 
indicate expertise." or "It is interesting to underline that during the 
experiment, the second user spontaneously declared that he felt “too 
stiff”, while the third user said that he “almost forgot about the robot”, 
which indicates a more relaxed behaviour." are note quite supported by 
quantitative evidence and this kind of hazardous extrapolation should be 
avoided (unless a clear definition of expertise is proposed or a clear 
explanation on what "feeling stiff" means is given). I suggest the authors 
to perform more experiements (and I know how difficult this is in the 
context of a pandemia) in order to obtain conclusive results.
