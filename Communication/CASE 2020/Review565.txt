Reviewer 3 of CASE 2020 submission 271

Comments to the author
======================

This paper presents a human-robot collaboration (HRC) setup
to estimate the impedance of human arm endpoint while
performing a representative cyclic task. Some preliminary
simulation results are reported using a couple of different
individuals to show the feasibility of the estimation task.

While the paper addresses an interesting problem that may
lead to biomimetic control of collaborative robots, it has
quite a few shortcomings. The major issues are listed
below:

- As discussed by the authors, several successful studies
have been conducted on human impedance estimation. In this
context, the specific contribution(s) of this work is/are
unclear. While the authors point out that they focus on the
interaction forces here instead of kinematic modeling as in
their previous works, it is hard to assess whether this
dynamics modeling offers any benefits over the estimation
methods developed by other research groups. 

- The experimental results are limited and unconvincing. As
acknowledged in the paper, no statistical significance
tests can be performed due to the extremely small number of
participants. Furthermore, the estimated stiffness value is
greater than what is reported in the literature. The
relative standard deviation of the average damping and mass
coefficients is also very high. All these raise questions
on the significance of the results. 

- It is hard to understand how the stability mapping
results are actually used to tune admittance control gains.


Minor points:

- There are quite a few grammatical errors in the text;
sentence construction is also awkward at several places.

- "practicable" should be practical in page 5

- "consistence" should be consistency in page 6
