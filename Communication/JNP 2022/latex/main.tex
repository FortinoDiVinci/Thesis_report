%	\section*{Introduction}
%	
%	Humans can tailor their limbs’ dynamic properties to achieve stable behavior with a wide range of environments while ensuring dexterous performances \parencite{burdet_central_2001,milner_impedance_2005,tsuji_bio-mimetic_2008}. %(Burdet et al. 2001; Milner and Franklin 2005; Tsuji and Tanaka 2008). 
%	Measuring the properties of the humans’ interactions during various tasks is relevant to understand the emergence of perturbed goal-directed movements. 
%	Moreover, reproducing these behaviors applies to the design of bio-inspired robot control and can also help gain insight into human motor control. 
%	Among limbs’ dynamic properties, the limbs’ mechanical impedance has been studied for decades under the control prism (Mussa-Ivaldi et al. 1985) and is still an active research topic (Börner et al. 2020; van de Ruit et al. 2021). 
%	Impedance relates kinematics to forces, allowing the modeling of human movements during physical interactions. 
%	Despite their limitations (Kistemaker and Rozendaal 2011), elementary models, such as mass-spring-damper models (KBM or KBI) or Hill-type muscle models, have been shown to approach human behavior (Tsuji and Tanaka 2008) and performances (Tee et al. 2004). 
%	These models do not intend to explain the complete human motor control but rather reproduce some of its aspects. More comprehensive models are required to analyze the neural processes underlying the control of dynamic limbs’ properties (Guarin and Kearney 2018; Sobhani Tehrani et al. 2017). 
%	Indeed, to account for passive muscle viscoelastic properties and active behaviors induced by reflexes and motion, Latash (1993) explained that a single joint would at least need 13 parameters, making it very sophisticated and almost impossible to study multiple joints impedance during movements. 
%	Less elaborated models at the endpoint level have been widely studied (Burdet et al. 2001; Dolan et al. 1993; Erden and Billard 2015a; Tsuji and Tanaka 2008), notably to study impedance during movements of multiple joints (Burdet et al. 2001; Erden and Billard 2015a). 
%	Decoupling simple models from biological considerations to study movements at the endpoint level with a behavioral approach even allowed to analyze performances, expertise (Erden and Billard 2015a), learning (Burdet et al. 2001; Tee et al. 2004), and exploration (Li et al. 2017).
%	
%	One of the main difficulties is that the mechanical impedance cannot be directly measured. 
%	To be estimated, it requires perturbing the limbs and comparing the observed movement to an unperturbed trajectory. 
%	Two configurations can be easily distinguished: static or isometric tasks (Dolan et al. 1993; Fang et al. 2018), where the unperturbed pose is well known, and dynamic tasks, where the estimation of the unperturbed trajectory is much more complex (Burdet et al. 2000; Gomi and Kawato 1997). 
%	When studying dynamic tasks, it can also be relevant to distinguish free movements without physical interaction with the environment from hybrid movements for which interactions with the environment are intermittent, potentially requiring time-varying mechanical impedance.
%	
%	Indeed, the study of impedance variations during movements and notably cyclic movements with contact phases and hybrid transitions is especially relevant for locomotion. 
%	The literature about ankle joint time-varying stiffness is rich during walking (Lee et al. 2016) or running (Shorter and Rouse 2020). 
%	Guarin (2018) also studied the reflex and intrinsic viscoelastic properties contributions of the ankle time-varying stiffness. 
%	To provide insights into the flexibility of human walking, Huang (2015) designed a Central Pattern Generator (CPG)-based human locomotion model, where the CPG model controlled not only joint torque but also joint stiffness (stiffness constant and equilibrium point). 
%	They improved the disturbance rejection from the ground ruggedness compared to previous models. 
%	The time-varying viscoelastic properties of the upper limbs have also been investigated in free cyclic movements (Bennett et al. 1992) and rhythmic movements with various loads (Li et al. 2019). 
%	Lacquaniti et al. (1993) and Tsuji (2008) studied the endpoint impedance of a ball-catching hybrid task (with both free movements and contact phases) with predictable physical interactions. 
%	
%	The present study characterizes the upper-limb mechanical impedance in a hybrid rhythmic task, such as a ball-bouncing task. 
%	The ball-bouncing task has already been studied for its apparent simplicity in order to address several motor control issues  (Ankarali et al. 2014; de Rugy et al. 2003; Siegler et al. 2010, 2013; Sternad et al. 2001). 
%	Avrin et al. (2017) implemented a CPG-based control structure to reproduce human-like kinematic performances in the ball bouncing. 
%	Yet, in the experimental data used to validate this model (Siegler et al. 2010, 2013), the dynamics of the physical interactions between the ball, the paddle, and the human were not simulated. 
%	For this purpose, a new experimental setup was designed using an admittance-controlled robot (Fortineau et al. 2020) that participants manipulated in order to vertically move a virtual paddle and bounce a virtual ball in a digital environment. 
%	The haptic feedback was also simulated during ball-paddle impacts. 
%	It is known to be relevant for performances as it reduces the noise in the state estimation of the ball (Ankarali et al. 2014). 
%	A methodology for endpoint impedance estimation during cyclic movements was also validated (Fortineau et al. 2021a, 2021b). 
%	Two experiments were conducted in order to characterize endpoint impedance in such an ecological multijoint rhythmic movement with low constraints generated by the robot. 
%	The first goal  was to assess whether endpoint-impedance parameters vary and follow a cyclic behavior. 
%	The second goal was to study the potential influence of the ball height to be reached on endpoint impedance and a possible relation between endpoint impedance parameters and performance level, as suggested by previous research on this topic (Erden and Billard 2015a).
